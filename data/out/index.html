ABSTRACT We reinvestigate Anderson, Reeb, and Zhao's findings that informed short selling is unique to family firms and driven by information leakage from internal disputes. Using the same firm sample and more comprehensive short‐selling data, we find that short selling predicts future returns in both family and nonfamily firms. We also find no significant difference in abnormal short selling before negative earnings announcements. In extended sample periods, short selling continues to predict returns for nonfamily firms under both ownership‐based and familial‐link definitions. Overall, our evidence suggests that short sellers are broadly informed and that family firms are not uniquely vulnerable.

Abstract This study assesses the effects of the 2009 Black Saturday Bushfires (BSB), which primarily occurred in rural regions of Victoria and represent the deadliest bushfire event in Australia's history, on household energy poverty. Using a linear panel event‐study, an entropy‐balanced event‐study, and a staggered difference‐in‐differences (DiD) design, applied to matched longitudinal household and geographical data, our results suggest a significant increase in the likelihood of experiencing energy poverty among households residing within 15 km of wildfire areas. Specifically, we find that for households directly affected by the fires, the likelihood of being in energy poverty increases by 9.9–11.3 percentage points in 2010 and by 13.2–16.7 percentage points in 2011 across specifications, compared to 2005–2007, which is the reference period. We find that the data are statistically consistent with life satisfaction, perceived safety and quality of life fully mediating the effect of BSB exposure on energy poverty in 2010, and with perceived safety fully mediating the relationship in 2011. We also find that adopting longer‐term financial planning mitigated the adverse effects of BSB exposure on energy poverty.

Abstract In past analyses of animal replacement decisions, studies have claimed that dairy farms often replace dairy cows too early to be maximizing profits. I estimate a structural model of asset replacement to determine the motives behind early replacement of dairy cows. I specifically test whether the costs from premature removal, an event where cows are removed off‐schedule due to health problems, explain replacement ages on dairy farms that are considered by previous studies to be unprofitable. Using data from over 1000 Wisconsin dairy farms, I find that a cow prematurely leaving the herd costs $2300 per removal, totaling $75 million in lost profit over the sample period. This cost is larger than what has been previously assumed, especially for dairies with less than 200 cows, suggesting that replacement rates appear unprofitable because previous analyses underestimated these costs. Dairy farms may benefit from prioritizing cow longevity and health over productivity to both cut costs and address environmental externalities from dairy. These results highlight the economic damages from poor cattle health on US dairy farms and the extent to which there may be cost savings from increasing cattle resilience to conditions that cause unexpected removal.

ABSTRACT This study proposes a time‐varying structural approximate dynamic factor (TVS‐ADF) model by extending the approximate dynamic factor (ADF) model in state‐space form. The TVS‐ADF model considers time‐varying coefficients and a time‐varying variance–covariance matrix of its innovation terms to capture complex economic dynamics. We propose the identification scheme of the common factors in the TVS‐ADF model and derive the identification theory. We also propose an effective Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) algorithm to estimate the TVS‐ADF model. To avoid the overparameterization caused by the time‐varying characteristics of the TVS‐ADF model, we include the shrinkage and sparsification approaches in the MCMC algorithm. Extensive simulation experiments and empirical applications demonstrate the outstanding performance of the TVS‐ADF model.
