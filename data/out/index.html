By 2024, China was importing over 70 percent of its crude oil, exposing the gasoline market to global volatility. Since 1998, the government has employed an administered pricing framework to shield growth and inflation. Using monthly data (June 2000–May 2025), this paper models China’s gasoline market to assess the ceiling’s mechanism and effectiveness. We find that (1) the ceiling mutes price spikes from negative supply and gasoline-specific shocks but tracks market responses to crude oil and macro demand shocks, a pattern consistent with the operation of centralized administrative reporting arrangements; (2) historical decompositions attribute meaningful stabilization to policy shocks in episodes such as February to November 2007 and May to August 2022; and (3) the counterfactual analysis identifies episodic deviations from the benchmark, particularly during major disruptions, while the magnitude of these deviations depends on the relative weights assigned to growth and inflation. According to all findings, we propose policy recommendations. We empirically examine the link between bank-specific innovations in financial technology (FinTech) and traditional banks’ fundamentals. We propose a new measure for FinTech innovation as the textual similarity of banks’ patent filings to standard FinTech policy documents. We show that FinTech innovation implies enhanced efficiency, profitability, and operating capacity (e.g., cost management, funding and lending activity). FinTech also ameliorates banks’ risk measures, including overall risk (Z-score), the liquidity, and nonperforming loan ratios. Our heterogeneity analysis suggests that banks with stronger managerial capacity and higher labor intensity derive moderately greater benefits from FinTech innovations. Further analysis reveals that FinTech-driven lending expansions focus on unsecured and low-risk loans, with technological specializations in cloud computing. We also find supportive evidence that expanding innovation in artificial intelligence and blockchain can yield performance gains to commercial banks. This paper was accepted by Kay Giesecke, finance. 展开完整摘要
Higher taxes on corporate payouts increase the cost of financing with stock issues. As a result, corporate investments that rely on external equity financing should be particularly sensitive to payout tax rates. A large literature shows that firms rely extensively on stock issues to fund research and development (R&D), suggesting that the financing of R&D is a potentially important microlevel mechanism linking payout taxation with real corporate activity. We document a robust negative association between dividend tax rates and firm R&D investment in a broad sample of developed countries between 2000 and 2019. The negative relation between dividend taxes and R&D is concentrated in equity-dependent firms in high-R&D industries. R&D is more sensitive to dividend tax rates in countries with stock market–based financial systems. Dividend taxes are less important for investment in physical capital than for R&D. Higher dividend taxes are associated with less corporate financing with stock issues, particularly in firms with low internal cash flow. Our study highlights the importance of focusing on equity-dependent activities and firms to evaluate the real consequences of payout taxation. This paper was accepted by Gustavo Manso, finance.
