展开完整摘要
Businesses in African countries face challenges not only with accessing electricity, but also with the high costs and unreliability of the power supply. To cope with frequent power outages and voltage fluctuations, many businesses often invest extra funds in power generators, alternative energy sources, and voltage stabilizers. These additional costs can represent a financial burden for businesses but are often overlooked when considering total electricity expenses. We explore the economic and electricity burden implications of unreliable power supply for businesses in Benin using national survey data on the power sector. We found that these extra costs make up a large part of businesses’ electricity expenses, with the main contributor being the expenses associated with running power generators. This places a heavy burden on many businesses, especially those operating in the informal sector and non-service businesses. Our findings imply that ignoring the additional electricity costs associated with defensive strategies results in underestimating the true financial burden of electricity expenditure for grid-connected businesses, which may lead to ineffective strategies for addressing electricity affordability in developing countries. Based on our results and since power outages and voltage fluctuations persist in most African countries, it is crucial to identify both the drivers and businesses experiencing electricity burden to implement targeted and evidence-informed alleviation measures. 展开完整摘要
We quantify the short-term impact of generative artificial intelligence (GenAI) on sales performance through a series of large-scale randomized field experiments involving millions of users and products at a leading crossborder online retail platform. Over 2023–2024, the platform integrated GenAI into seven business workflows spanning customer service, consumer-product matching, advertising, and seller services. We find that GenAI adoption increases sales in most workflows, with effects ranging from no detectable impact to 16.3% depending on GenAI’s marginal contribution relative to baseline firm practices. Across the four GenAI applications with positive sales effects, the implied annual incremental value is roughly $5 per consumer—an economically meaningful impact given the retailer’s scale and the early stage of GenAI adoption. The gains operate primarily through higher conversion rates rather than larger cart values, consistent with GenAI improving the shopping experience by reducing search, information, communication, and personalization frictions. Importantly, these effects are not associated with worse postpurchase outcomes as product return rates and customer ratings do not deteriorate. Finally, we document substantial demand-side heterogeneity, with larger gains for less experienced consumers. Our findings provide novel, large-scale causal evidence on how GenAI shapes sales productivity in online retail, highlighting both its immediate value and broader potential.
