**摘要**

Abstract The quality of investors’ information across different horizons has real effects. ## 6. Effortful vs. Opportunistic Consumption of Discretionary Time: How Time-Reporting Decisions Influence Extra-Role Effort

**Management Science** · 上线 2026-11-12（1 天前） · T1 · Sophie Maussen · 综合分 **37.9** · 强相关

**摘要**

Employees often work under incomplete contracts, performing both contracted in-role tasks and valuable but noncontracted extra-role activities. Once employees have completed in-role tasks, discretionary time remains, which they may allocate to extra-role activities or consume opportunistically. This study examines how managers’ time-reporting decisions, as a less intrusive form of control, affect subordinates’ effort-allocation decisions. I propose two opposing predictions. First, managers’ control implementations may signal distrust, resulting in more opportunism and thus less extra-role effort. Second, I identify value signaling as a novel theoretical mechanism through which time-reporting decisions may increase extra-role effort. The main experiment manipulated two factors: the type of time report (aggregated versus disaggregated) and its implementation source (managerial choice versus random assignment) to isolate signaling effects from monitoring effects. Results reveal that when managers choose not to implement disaggregated time reporting—that is, reports that make discretionary time salient—subordinates significantly reduce their extra-role effort. Structural equation modeling shows that value signaling outweighs distrust signaling in driving employees’ effort-allocation decisions. In contrast, when the time-reporting format is randomly assigned, effort remains unchanged, underscoring the signaling role of managerial choice. An additional online experiment shows directionally consistent results with the main experiment. These findings reveal a hidden cost of not controlling and contribute to the literature on incomplete contracts, slack resources, and input-based controls in modern work contexts. This paper was accepted by Ranjani Krishnan, accounting. Funding: This work was supported by the Bijzonder Onderzoeksfonds UGent [Grant BOF/STA/202009/047]. Supplemental Material: The data files are available at https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2025.00955 .

## 8. Labor Market Tightness and the Reversal of Inequality Trends in Germany

**The Quarterly Journal of Economics** · 上线 2026-11-10（3 天前） · 在线 2026-11-11 · T1 · Christian Dustmann、Martin Friedrich、Carl Gergs、Uta Schönberg · 综合分 **35.6** · 弱相关

标签：`wage`

**摘要**

Abstract Germany experienced a sharp rise in wage inequality over the two decades leading up to the Great Recession. However, a remarkable trend reversal occurred in 2010—well before the introduction of the minimum wage in 2015—when wages at the bottom of the distribution began to grow more rapidly than those higher up. This paper attributes the reversal to an increasingly tight labor market after the Great Recession, supported by strong domestic demand, and operating through several interrelated and mutually reinforcing channels. Consistent with this hypothesis, job-to-job transition rates and between-job wage growth rose markedly after the recession, especially for workers at the lower rungs of the job ladder. Concurrently, workers increasingly moved from low-paying to higher-paying firms, compressing the worker-weighted distribution of firm wage premia. Tighter labor markets also reduced assortative matching between workers and firms, lowered the skill premium, and weakened firms’ monopsony power.

**摘要**

This paper explores how venture capitalists (VCs) add value by examining their role as labor market intermediaries. Using novel data, I show that VCs create internal labor markets (ILMs) to facilitate entrepreneurial mobility across their portfolio firms. Survey evidence suggests that VCs leverage private information about founders’ abilities to strategically match talent to portfolio firms, creating value for the recipient companies. Consistent with this mechanism of asymmetric learning, I find that (a) former VC partners continue to influence founder mobility, (b) ILM intensity positively predicts VC fund returns, and (c) the ILM effect is stronger when information asymmetry is greater. **摘要**

We construct novel network-based measures of U.S. state-level bank deregulation intensity that allow us to separately identify the effects of deregulation on competition and investment opportunities. In contrast to existing studies, we find that increased competition leads to higher deposit funding costs and a reduction in banks’ net interest margins and profitability. In response, banks shift their business models and persistently increase their risk taking. Our findings resolve conflicting evidence in the literature and support theories in which reductions in bank charter values lead to higher bank risk taking.
