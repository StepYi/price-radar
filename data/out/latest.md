## 5. Competition and Diversity in Generative AI

**Management Science** · 上线 2026-10-16（2 天前） · T1 · Manish Raghavan · 综合分 **35.1** · 强相关

**摘要**

Recent evidence, both in the laboratory and in the wild, suggests that the use of generative artificial intelligence reduces the diversity of content produced. As generative AI penetrates more markets and reaches widespread adoption, should we still expect to see content homogenization? Our work begins with a simple observation: content producers do not act in isolation. Instead, they compete with one another (e.g., for customers or attention). We explore the impact competition has on both content diversity and overall social welfare when producers use generative AI. Through a formal game-theoretic model, we show that competitive markets select for diverse AI models, mitigating monoculture. We further show that a generative AI model that performs well in isolation (i.e., according to a benchmark) may fail to provide value in a competitive market. Our results highlight the importance of evaluating generative AI models across the breadth of their output distributions, particularly when they will be deployed in competitive environments. We validate our theoretical results with a series of experiments involving generative text and image models. Overall, our results suggest that homogenization due to generative AI is unlikely to persist under strong competition. ## 8. Editor’s Introduction: Bridging Research and Practice: Policy Insights for the Evolving Electricity Sector

**The Energy Journal** · 上线 2026-10-09（9 天前） · 在线 2026-10-08 · T2 · Lynne Kiesling、David Rapson · 综合分 **33.6** · 中等相关

标签：`electricity` `renewable`

**摘要**

This special issue collects six articles that translate economic research into practical guidance for electricity regulators. The articles cover regulated monopoly structure, the economics of regulation after restructuring, electric vehicle integration in constrained distribution networks, distributed backup power and grid reliability, the spatial and temporal variation in the environmental value of renewable energy, and energy justice. Across these domains, legacy regulatory frameworks designed for an earlier technological era now generate predictable distortions. Three themes recur: institutional lag, or the pacing problem; the dispersed and tacit knowledge that no central authority can fully aggregate; and the tension between efficiency and equity. Together, the contributions show that economic analysis can inform the decisions regulators face, provided researchers write for practitioners and practitioners' institutional experience shapes the questions researchers ask.
